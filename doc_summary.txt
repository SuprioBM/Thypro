Company and opportunity

What Thypro is, why now, who pays, and how it reaches its first customers.

01  Thypro at a glance

A shared definition for founders, product, engineering, clinical and operating teams.

One-line description

Thypro is a person-owned, lifelong health record with an AI care layer that acts on it, so patients, their families wherever they live, and their clinics can manage care together in their own language.

Health context today is scattered across paper prescriptions, lab PDFs, clinic systems and phone calls, and the people who coordinate care are often not in the same room, city or country as the patient. Thypro gives each person one portable, source-labelled record and connects it to the people and services who act on it: family members and caregivers, clinics, labs, pharmacies, field agents, dietitians and blood services. Governed AI organises, reminds, summarises, plans and handles administrative work. Verified people make clinical decisions and complete in-person tasks.

The vision is global and multi-sided. The same record and permission layer serves a person managing their own health, a daughter abroad coordinating her father's care, a clinic running its front desk, a blood bank finding a verified donor, and in the long run a family that keeps its health history across generations. Care for ageing parents is one of the most urgent jobs this layer does and the first one families will pay for, but it is a feature of the platform rather than its boundary.

The design combines the functional patterns of HealthLeap, PocketPatientMD, Nolla Health and Confido Health. Adoption means independently implementing an appropriate capability or business pattern. It does not transfer their code, models, datasets, brand names, clinical evidence, certifications, contracts, permissions or revenue. Every retained capability has an implementation requirement or a documented decision in the traceability appendix.

02  Vision: a lifelong, generational health record

Where Thypro goes after the first products work, stated honestly.

A person's health history should not disappear when they change doctors, move countries or die. Thypro's long-term aim is a record that lasts a lifetime and can be passed on: grandchildren should be able to know that diabetes or heart disease runs in their family, and a person should be able to decide what their heirs receive. In a hundred or two hundred years, people should still be able to learn the health history of their ancestors if those ancestors chose to share it.

No company can honestly promise to exist for two hundred years, so Thypro promises something it can keep: the record outlives Thypro. That rests on four requirements, all specified in section 11.

What Thypro deliberately does not do

No blockchain storage of health data. Data on a blockchain cannot be corrected or deleted, which conflicts with legal rights to correction and erasure and with Thypro's own correction, deletion and revocation rules (REC10, WEAR06, consent grants). Thypro keeps the goal behind the idea, tamper-evident records the person owns, through audit trails, provenance and TRU07 integrity proofs. See decision D1 in section 22.

No open ancestor search. A relative's diagnosis reveals risk about living family members who never agreed to share it, and health information about deceased people remains protected; in the United States the HIPAA Privacy Rule protects a decedent's identifiable health information for 50 years after death. [Y5] Family history is shared only through consented links (REC12) and legacy rules (REC11). See decision D2.

No claim to be the only or first platform. Thypro's differentiation is a hypothesis tested through retention, completed tasks and trust (section 07).

03  Fit with YC Requests for Startups

Primary: AI for the Aging Population

The request responds to a growing older population and a shrinking caregiver workforce, and points to software such as voice check-ins, care coordination and family visibility. Secondary: Proving You're Human

This request is about rebuilding trust as deepfakes flood calls, messages and transactions. Older people are a primary target: according to FBI IC3 figures for 2025, people aged 60 and over reported about $7.7 billion in losses across more than 201,000 complaints, roughly 37% of all reported losses. [Y3,Y6] Thypro Trust verifies the people at the door and on the phone, checks suspicious calls with independent call-backs, and asks family to confirm risky actions. It is a module of Thypro, not a separate identity company.

Not claimed

Data for the Real World asks for companies that collect dense physical-world data with fleets of robots, balloons or sensors. [Y1] Thypro does not, and presenting its records as a data asset would contradict its rule against selling identifiable health data.

Self-Maintaining APIs asks for agents that scan customers' codebases and open fixes when a vendor ships a breaking API change. [Y1] Thypro needs resilient device and EHR adapters (CON08, WEAR01–08), but that is internal engineering, not what Thypro sells.

04  Problems, evidence and why now

The problems that justify Thypro and how each will be tested.

Problem

Evidence and practical implication

Thypro response

Fragmented health context

Records, lab PDFs, prescription photos and device summaries arrive from different systems. A portable record must distinguish source records from patient entries; interoperability cannot be assumed. [P1,P3,R7]

Source labeled timeline, reviewed extraction, exports, consented sharing and adapters.

Gaps in access and affordability

WHO estimates 4.6 billion people lacked full essential service coverage in 2023 and 2.1 billion faced financial hardship from health spending in 2022. These are different populations and years, not additive paying users. [M1]

Low bandwidth access, transparent costs, assisted use and locally available care pathways. Software cannot itself supply missing clinicians or medicines.

Administrative delay

Scheduling, referrals, coverage checks, missing information and payment follow up recur across comparator offerings. Their marketing indicates demand but does not quantify every clinic’s loss. [C2–C7]

Owned tasks, scheduling rules, documented handoffs and measurable workflow completion.

Missed clinical signals

HealthLeap’s published malnutrition research supports investigating record based screening in a defined adult inpatient setting. It does not validate Thypro or every clinical condition. [H3,H4]

Separate research and clinical intelligence program, beginning with licensed data and local prospective evaluation.

Unconnected device data

Consumer devices produce useful but uneven observations. Vendor metrics, permissions and missingness differ. They do not replace clinical measurements. [W1,W4,W10,W12]

Optional connections, provenance, metric definitions and trend views with explicit limits.

Blood request uncertainty

WHO reports 120.4 million donations in its 2026 fact sheet, with marked differences in collection rates across income groups. Availability also depends on collection, screening, storage and distribution. [M4,M5]

Verified need requests and donor coordination linked to blood banks, with freshness, privacy and completion tracking.

Digital exclusion

ITU estimated 6 billion people online in 2025 and 2.2 billion offline. Smartphone, literacy and wearable ownership cannot be assumed. [M2]

Manual records, caregiver and staff assistance, accessible design, localized text and deferred synchronization.

Care at a distance

Families increasingly coordinate care for relatives in another city or country. Bangladesh received a record US$35.56 billion in remittances in FY2025-26, up 17.3%, showing the scale of family support sent from abroad. [Y7] Company claim: more than half of Indian professionals aged 40–55 living abroad are caught between ageing parents at home and their own families. [Y4]

Family circle, daily check-ins, coordinated tasks and verified in-person help (Family Care).

Ageing populations

By 2030 one in six people will be aged 60 or over; the 80-plus population is expected to triple between 2020 and 2050 to 426 million. [Y2]

Voice-first, low-literacy-friendly access, medication and appointment support, caregiver delegation.

Scams and impersonation

People aged 60 and over reported about $7.7 billion in losses to the FBI in 2025. [Y3,Y6] AI voice cloning makes impersonation cheap.

Verified people, scam checks with independent call-back, family confirmation for risky actions (Trust).

Lost family health history

Hypothesis: family history is a routine clinical risk factor, yet it rarely survives in a portable, shareable form across generations.

Family Health Tree, digital legacy and lifelong record continuity (REC11–13).

Generic health advice

Hypothesis: people receive one-size advice that ignores their records, conditions, culture and schedule, and rarely get help choosing the right specialist.

Personal plans within safety limits, clinician-approved condition plans and specialist suggestions (Plans).

Why now

Four changes make this buildable by a small team now. Language models can draft structured fields from a photographed prescription and hold a natural voice conversation in many languages at a cost that fits a consumer subscription. Phones expose health data through governed native APIs such as Android Health Connect. [W4] Deepfake voice and video have made identity on a phone call unreliable, raising the value of verified people. And more families than ever coordinate care across borders.

Discovery that must precede product commitments

Interview patients, caregivers, front desk staff, clinicians, clinic owners, blood bank operators and hospital informatics teams separately. Observe the current workflow and collect baseline task volume, time, abandonment, error rate and cost with permission. Ask which system is authoritative, who owns the next action and which failure causes real harm. A stated interest is weaker evidence than a paid pilot, data access agreement or repeated use.

Initial discovery target is an assumption: 20 to 30 interviews across the first clinic segment, 10 patient or caregiver usability sessions, three blood service partner workflow reviews and two wearable integration spikes. These are learning activities rather than statistically representative market surveys. Publish findings, rejected assumptions and changes to the backlog before finalizing a commercial promise.

For Family Care and Plans, add 20 interviews with family members abroad, 10 with parents or relatives at home, and five reviews with clinicians or dietitians on plan safety rules (assumption).

Localization, languages and offline use

Support UTC storage and IANA time zones, Unicode names, multiple scripts, flexible addresses, international phone numbers, local date formats, metric and customary units, currencies and tax rules. Country configuration includes legal entity, data region, eligible ages, provider credentials, emergency contacts, communication consent and permitted features. No country can silently inherit another country’s prescribing authority.

Thypro is multilingual by design. Language support is configured per country and per workflow, so a language can be live for reminders before it is live for clinical summaries. Voice recognition and synthesis are evaluated per language and accent; document extraction is tested on real prescriptions in each script; clinical terms are human-reviewed and drug names are never translated into a different product. A parent can use one language while relatives abroad read summaries in another, with the original wording preserved. English and Bangla come first, followed by languages such as Hindi, Urdu, Nepali, Arabic and Tamil as corridors open (proposal). Thypro will not claim parity across dozens of languages or worldwide operations because a comparator advertises it. Accessible keyboard navigation, screen reader labels, high contrast, scalable type and non-map alternatives target WCAG 2.2 AA. [R6]

The web application supports essential records and clinic work. Native iOS and Android components are required for the relevant on-device health integrations. Offline work is limited initially to an encrypted patient selected summary and queued patient entries on trusted devices; no offline prescribing, payment commitment, slot confirmation or public donor contact cache. Resolve synchronization conflicts explicitly. Shared devices default to no persistent clinical cache.

09  Go-to-market and 30-day traction plan

Start where trust and willingness to pay are highest, then expand within the network.

Families abroad are the first paying users: they feel the problem sharply, pay in strong currencies and find each other through community groups, student and worker associations and word of mouth. Clinics join as design partners that receive referrals and structured records, which builds the supply side. Field fulfilment in Dhaka runs through the BringMe errand network. Expansion follows corridors (new country pairs) and deepens within existing families and clinics before adding unrelated specialties.

For clinics, use founder led sales to the design-partner segment and recruit a small design partner cohort with baseline workflow data and a named operational champion. Demonstrate a complete record to appointment to follow up journey. Define pilot scope, support hours, data responsibility and exit export. Charge for value once the workflow is stable; free pilots should have a time limit and explicit learning objective.

Expand within successful organizations before adding many unrelated specialties. Partner channels may include clinic networks, laboratories, pharmacies, hospitals, blood services and NGO programs, with clear independence and data permissions. Consumer adoption can come through trusted care interactions and consented referrals. Track activation, week four and week twelve retention and completed care tasks rather than downloads alone.

30-day plan

Week 1: interview 20 families abroad and 10 parents or relatives at home about what they did and spent in the last three months; recruit three design-partner clinics.

Week 2: run a concierge version of Family Care for the first families, with check-ins, reminders and summaries partly done by hand behind the product.

Week 3: charge a pilot price, complete the first verified field tasks, and log every coordinator and call minute.

Week 4: review retention, task completion and costs; decide what to automate next and what to stop.

Data contract and ingestion rules

Store a canonical Observation with tenant and patient IDs, source account, original event ID, metric code, original value and unit, normalized value and unit, effective start and end, time zone, ingestion time, source device and method, quality flags, transformation version and consent reference. Preserve original payload only under a justified retention policy. Use explicit codes for missing, unsupported, stale and invalid data; none equals zero. [R7]

Acceptance scenarios and operations

Two sources mirror one workout: only one preferred activity contributes to the daily aggregate, with both lineage links retained.

A user crosses a time zone or daylight saving boundary: raw times remain unchanged and local day aggregation follows the documented rule.

A vendor revokes access or returns 429: retrieval stops or backs off, and the UI shows stale data rather than a fabricated zero.

A source corrects or deletes an event: downstream trends recalculate and the audit records the change.

A user disconnects: future collection stops; retention and deletion choices are presented, with any legally retained clinical copy explained.

A clinician has no sharing grant: the patient’s connected account remains invisible to that clinician. A clinic relationship alone does not grant every device scope.

Health trends can support wellness discussion and clinician review. Workflow and state ownership

A request begins as Draft. Staff verification moves it to Open, or rejects it with a reason. Open requests can enter Inviting; donor acceptance creates an Accepted response and then an Appointment. A verified collection partner records Confirmed donation. The request can also become Partially fulfilled, Fulfilled, Unfulfilled, Cancelled or Expired. Multiple donor responses attach to one request; one acceptance must not close a multi-unit need. Every transition records actor, timestamp, reason and request version.

Candidates are ordered by self reported or verified group relevance, coarse distance, availability, contact preferences, recent outreach burden and partner rules. This is outreach relevance, not clinical compatibility. A blood bank determines donor eligibility, collection suitability, testing, component compatibility and crossmatching. No AI model, blood group label or wearable measurement certifies transfusion safety. [M4,M5]

Send staged invitations to avoid mass spam and excess travel. Give the donor a decline or pause action, travel instructions, center hours and an authorized contact relay. Expire unanswered availability and the underlying need automatically. If a partner cannot confirm capacity, show the request as unresolved and offer verified contact routes. Do not imply an ambulance dispatch or guaranteed emergency response.

Map privacy, abuse and reliability

No donor names, phone numbers, exact homes, device locations or sensitive medical fields in public map responses, vector tiles, analytics or caches.

Use coarse area indexing and thresholded counts; restrict coordinator access and log every contact reveal. Do not allow bulk scraping through search pagination.

Verify requests with a responsible organization; detect duplicates, impossible quantities, repeated contact harvesting, money demands and suspicious repeated accounts. Provide report, suspend, appeal and reviewer assignment actions.

Define freshness limits per partner rather than invent one universal stock expiry. Display last update and operating hours; hide or label stale inventory.

Use relay messaging and rate limits, with local consent and quiet hours. An emergency label alone does not authorize contacting every donor.

Never sell blood, rank donors by ability to pay or charge a scarcity commission. Institutional software and sponsorship can support coordination without charging people to volunteer.

Release a geographic zone only after at least one verified collection or hospital partner has agreed to request verification, contact coverage, incident handling and confirmation. Test false requests, revoked consent, no GPS, stale stock, expired needs, duplicate notifications and partial fulfillment. Measure verified requests, time to first qualified response, appointments, partner confirmed donations, unfulfilled need rate, opt outs and abuse. None is a direct measure of lives saved without an appropriate study.

Each adapter declares system of record, authentication, approved scopes, resource mappings, source identifiers, rate limits, webhook verification, replay behavior, backfill, conflict resolution, deletion semantics, error ownership and support contact. If AI is unavailable, records and manual clinic workflows remain usable. Concrete controls and country-specific review are part of the product specification.

Consumer health services may still fall within FTC breach requirements. GDPR health data processing requires an appropriate lawful basis and special category condition as well as safeguards. Revoking a sharing grant stops future access. It cannot make a clinician forget information already read or automatically erase a legally retained clinical record. The interface must explain this without implying that all records are deletable on demand. Device disconnect, account deletion and withdrawal from optional research are separate actions.

Clinical and country release assessment

Classify each function by intended use, user, output, decision impact and human review. FDA CDS guidance and European medical software guidance illustrate that classification depends on function; a disclaimer does not make diagnostic or treatment software nonmedical. Determine local device, professional practice, pharmacy, telemedicine, blood service, data and consumer obligations before release. [R1,R5] Maintain a country register rather than one global legal checkbox.

Clinical partners approve protocols, emergency escalation, adverse event reporting, prescriptions, pharmacy arrangements, clinician credential checks, patient location checks and minors or guardianship rules. Restricted services should be unavailable when eligibility or partner coverage cannot be established. A patient can still use permitted nonclinical functions. Do not make claims such as HIPAA certified, FDA approved, ISO certified or clinically proven until the exact claim is supported by current evidence and scope.

Personal plans for listed conditions, diet advice and glucose logging are treated as clinical functions for this assessment, not as general wellness.

Priority threat scenarios

An employee or model guesses another patient ID: policy and tenant checks deny access and record the attempt.

A donor map is scraped: public responses contain no individual donor coordinates, and abuse controls limit enumeration.

A malicious report instructs the assistant to export records: untrusted content cannot modify allowed tools or authorization.

A lost shared phone contains cached data: encrypted minimal cache, short sessions and remote revoke limit exposure.

A vendor outage delays medication or referral routing: an owned task and staff escalation prevent silent loss.

A wrong patient merge is proposed: manual review, reversible linkage and immutable provenance prevent destructive consolidation.

A caller clones a relative's voice to request money or records: TRU03 call-back and TRU05 family confirmation interrupt the request.

A field agent is impersonated at the door: TRU02 one-time code blocks hand-over.

Health records and AI care for families, anywhere.  (50 characters)

What is your company going to make?

Thypro gives people one health record they own for life and an AI care layer that acts on it. A patient or a family member photographs prescriptions and reports; Thypro turns them into a reviewed timeline, builds medication schedules, books appointments and checks in by voice every day in the person's own language. C  Glossary and change control

Shared meanings for implementation and future revisions.

Term

Meaning in this document

Person controlled record

A person can view, contribute, export and delegate access subject to law and the provenance of clinical records; this is not unlimited edit authority over clinician notes.

Provenance

The origin, time, method, transformation and review history of information.

Consent grant

A specific documented permission; effective access also depends on identity, role, purpose, scope and law.

Clinical decision support

Software supporting a clinical decision with an intended use that requires specific evaluation and possibly regulatory obligations.

AUROC and AUPRC

Discrimination measures; neither means the percentage of patients correctly diagnosed. Interpretation depends on prevalence, threshold and setting.

TAM SAM and SOM

Total addressable, serviceable available and serviceable obtainable markets, with explicit customer definitions and assumptions.

ARR and GMV

Recurring platform revenue annualized under a stated policy versus gross transaction value; these are not interchangeable.

FHIR and HL7

Healthcare interoperability standards; actual compatibility requires profiles, versions, mappings and partner tests.

M and E

Monitoring and evaluation for a defined program, with approved indicators and data access.

Silent evaluation

A model is tested prospectively without directing routine clinical decisions.

Release gate

Documented evidence and accountable approval needed before a proposed function is made available.

Family circle

The people a person has invited to help with their care, each with a role, scope and expiry.

Check-in

A scheduled AI call or message asking about medicines, wellbeing and needs; not monitoring.

Field agent

A verified person who completes an in-person task such as delivery or accompaniment.

Corridor

A pair of countries served together, such as relatives in Bangladesh and family members in the UK.

Digital legacy

A person's rules for what named contacts receive after their death.

Family Health Tree

Consented links between relatives sharing only the conditions each chooses.

Integrity proof

A cryptographic fingerprint and timestamp showing a document has not changed; contains no health data.

Condition plan

A personal plan for someone with a listed condition; requires clinician or dietitian approval.

Future changes record date, decision owner, reason, supporting evidence, affected requirements, user impact and migration or rollback needs. Maintain a living backlog and country matrix alongside this document; do not overwrite the historical basis for a clinical or data decision.
